Where shoplifting reports concentrate

Shoplifting reports cluster hard on a few store-corners. This map shows the absolute number of reports per corner for the window you pick below — bigger circle, more reports. It is a snapshot of concentration, not a “what moved” comparison.

Top corners in this window

Read a hot corner as one store, not one block. A shoplifting corner is usually a single retailer, so its count reflects that store’s loss-prevention reporting — which can switch on or off (a new manager, a reporting drive) without the underlying theft changing. Corners are placed by the incident report’s native intersection; shoplifting only.

How this is measured — data & method

  • Source: SFPD Incident Reports 2018→present (wg3w-h783), updated daily. We track two merchant-theft types — shoplifting [1] and commercial burglary & robbery [2] — and exclude one legacy code [3]. Each footnote explains why and links the exact query used. District from the native police_district field (spot-checked in Northern: ~99% consistent with point-in-polygon).
  • Window: 2021 onward. 2020 (pandemic store closures) distorted both retail theft and the citywide denominators, so it's excluded as an anomalous baseline; charts, averages, and the “all-time” share all start in 2021.
  • The two axes [4]. Reported by businesses = incidents resolved Open or Active (a victim/merchant report, no on-scene arrest) — the success metric, robust to enforcement. Arrests = Cite or Arrest Adult — shown as context, not a target.
  • Enforcement caution. Shoplifting arrest counts track enforcement intensity, not theft — a 2024 crackdown spiked arrests ~7× while business reports barely moved. That's why arrests are shown beside reports, never as the success metric alone.
  • The dark-figure caveat. Reported counts depend on businesses choosing to report. A drop could mean less theft or reporting fatigue. Framing is observational, not causal.
