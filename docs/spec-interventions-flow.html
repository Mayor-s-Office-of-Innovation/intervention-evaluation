Screen 1 · District home

OKRs and Interventions become scrolling sections

Today a district hides Interventions behind a tab. Make OKRs a section you land on, then scroll to Interventions below it — and rename the call-to-action so adding an intervention reads as its own action.

  • 10No more tab. OKRs is a labeled section at the top; Interventions sits directly below it on scroll. The bottom card changes from "Explore interventions" to "Add a new intervention," mirroring the existing "Explore" pattern but naming the action.
  • 1Two entry points, one place to manage. Add lands here from the tool (Screen 3); this Interventions section is where you monitor and edit them afterward (Screen 2).

Screen 2 · Interventions list

Monitor, edit, and close — with who and when

The list each district scrolls to — shown here with Central's real interventions. It carries every field from the current Excel tracker, adds accountability columns, lets you edit a row to monitor it, and closes rather than deletes, with closed ones still findable.

  • 2Every field from the current Excel tracker. ID · Intervention · Description (problem statement) · Evidence · Tactics · Owner · Associated agencies · Levers (expected change) · Status · Start date · End date · Roadblock / flag · Outcomes · Notes. The list shows a readable subset; the rest open on the row. Status values match theirs: Open · In progress, Closed · Completed, At risk · On hold.
  • 11Person submitted + Last edited. Two net-new columns (not in the Excel) so every intervention shows who logged it and when it last changed — distinct from Owner.
  • 12Close, don't delete. The trash action becomes Close — which sets Status to Closed · Completed and stamps an End date (and Outcomes). Closed rows leave the active list but stay reachable via "View closed interventions," and can be reopened.
  • 1Edit = monitor. Editing a row reopens its full record (and its measurement deep dive) to update status, outcomes, or details over time. The dashboard also adds a live Impact read the Excel can't compute.

Screen 3 · Add an intervention (from the hypothesis tool)

The add form should feel like the tracker

Reframe "Does the data support my hypothesis?" as adding an intervention. Bring in every field from the current Excel tracker (shown here pre-filled with Central's CEN002), open up the Levers picker, allow a tiny radius, support a photo, and make the button say what it does.

  • 7Mirror the real Excel tracker. Every field from the Central tracker appears here and is editable — Intervention, Description (problem statement), Evidence, Levers, Tactics, Owner, Associated agencies, Status, Start/End date, Roadblock/flag, Outcomes, Notes — so adding in the tool and adding in the tracker are the same form.
  • 6Levers open to any variable with data. "What do you expect to change" becomes the Levers field — directional outcomes like ↓ Animal incidents or ↑ Public space use — no longer a short fixed list, and selectable for every signal we hold data on (multi-select, searchable).
  • 5Name the action. The primary button becomes "Submit intervention" (replacing "Check the data"), so it's clear you're creating something, not just running a query.
  • 4Smaller radius. Lower the minimum well below the current 100 m so a single corner (e.g. Post & Stockton) can be logged precisely. The mock shows a 10 m floor as a placeholder; see open items below for the value to confirm.
  • 8Attachments. Let the submitter attach a photo of the work done (and other files).
  • 2ID is auto-assigned in the district's sequence (CEN009 here), matching the tracker's ID convention.

Screen 4 · Monitoring the impact

Timeline marker stays; the summary speaks to the date

Keep the "created on" marker on the metric you're testing, and rewrite the AI summary so its timing is anchored to when the intervention was logged.

  • 3Keep the marker. The intervention's creation date stays drawn on the timeline of the exact metric you're testing, so the before/after is visible at a glance.
  • 9Anchor the summary to the date. Rewrite it to read "since you logged this on [date], here's what's changed" — comparing the period after the intervention to the period before it, instead of a vague rolling window.
